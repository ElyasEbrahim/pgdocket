# Can asking rows twice catch changes in no one direction?

No API calls. Generated by `jevbench drifttwice` (#37) from real asks. With `--twice` a row without a label is asked twice: of its stored answer and the two new ones, when exactly two agree, the odd one is the stored answer a third of the time if nothing changed. Evidence that never expires bets on it being the stored one, on a share of the half of the evidence budget that needs no labels. 1,000 series per scenario, 4,000 without a change.

**Outcome.** Asking twice is not built. It caught what no other check does (criterion 2), and simulated series without a change stayed quiet, but criterion 1 is not met as written: on the real CFPB repeats, one of the three ways to pair them rejects. The criterion did not say how to treat three pairings, so the strict reading is applied. A replication on fresh repeats, with one test fixed beforehand, would settle it.

## The criteria fixed before the first run

1. Real repeats: 1 of the 4 tests above rejected at 5 % (smallest p = 0.011): not passed as written. Simulated series without a change: at most 1.5 % alarmed: passed.
2. Shuffled batches on every row from the first check, by the last check, at the same calls: asked twice 100.0 %, asked once 2.4 %: passed.
3. Every other scenario at the same calls: 9 losses above 5 points: failed, so asking twice would be an option, not the default.
4. Share of the label-free half of the evidence budget: **0.50**. Reserving it costs rows asked once at most 3.4 points in any scenario.
5. The same test on scores, half the rows asked twice: shuffled batches by the last check 100.0 %; the other scenarios are in the tables' last column.
