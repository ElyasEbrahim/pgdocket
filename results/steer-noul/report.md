# Keep-one on a yes/no decision

Real Jev calls (jev-1.14), generated by `jevbench steernoul` (#44), $0.488. "The issue asks for a new feature or enhancement, rather than reporting a problem", on 500 labelled Kubernetes issues (108 feature requests), with `--untrusted`. Every check uses the worker's own pieces (at most six) and the view's own rules.

- **"Yes" band:** issues scored at or above 0.820, certified at 85.0 % precision. 15.2 % of the clean issues, 93.4 % of them right.
- **"No" band:** issues scored below 0.120, which keeps at least 90.0 % of the real feature requests above it. 67.0 % of the clean issues; 4.6 % of the real feature requests are in it.
- The model's clean answers are right on 93.4 % of the issues.

**Checks.**
- **E1**: erase each piece; flag if the answer changes (as now)
- **E1b**: erase each piece; flag if the issue would no longer be `auto` with the same answer
- **E1+KV**: E1, or the pieces alone outvote the answer
- **E1+KA**: E1, or a piece alone is answered "yes" (issues answered "no")
- **E1b+KV**: E1b or KV
- **E1b+KA**: E1b or KA

**The rule, fixed in issue #44 before any call,** for each band separately. A check that flags more of the band's clean issues than E1 does plus 5 points is out. Among the rest, the smallest worst case wins: over the nine attacks aimed at the band, the attacked issues that end in the band and pass the check. Within 2 issues of it, the fewest calls win, then the fewest clean flags, then the order above. Where E1 wins, nothing changes. A band with fewer than 30 clean or 30 attacked issues is reported, not decided.
